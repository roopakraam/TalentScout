# Abstract

Football clubs must judge which young players will become elite from limited evidence, and a scout cannot measure every attribute of every player. This project uses ten seasons of EA Sports FIFA and FC ratings (FIFA 15 to FC 24) to predict which outfield players aged 21 or under and rated below 80 reach an 80+ overall rating within three game versions, and to find the smallest set of the game's 45 attributes that predicts it. Feature selection is formulated as a binary optimization problem with a weighted fitness of validation PR-AUC and subset size. A Genetic Algorithm, Binary Particle Swarm Optimization and a Binary Grey Wolf Optimizer are compared with random search under an equal budget of 600 evaluations, shared starting populations and ten seeds; model hyperparameters are tuned by Bayesian optimization (TPE). The data are split by season and grouped by player, with the newest season held out as a test set used once. All three metaheuristics beat random search on every seed (Wilcoxon, Holm-adjusted p = 0.012) without differing significantly from each other; Binary GWO converged fastest and kept the fewest attributes. On validation the selected subsets of 16 to 25 attributes scored far above all 45 (PR-AUC 0.61 to 0.63 against 0.50). On the test season, every model scored 0.28 to 0.35 PR-AUC, subsets matched the full attribute set within noise, and no model beat EA's potential rating for the 80+ target (LightGBM minus potential: −0.026, 95% CI −0.116 to 0.059), although LightGBM did for a 75+ target (+0.080, CI +0.036 to +0.126). The validation gains came from a validation set made easier by the split design and from selection fitting noise; a cross-validation fallback did no better. Compact scouting checklists lose nothing, but validation-driven selection gains must be checked on untouched data.

**Keywords:** feature selection, genetic algorithm, binary particle swarm optimization, grey wolf optimizer, Bayesian optimization, talent identification, football analytics
