# Abstract

Football clubs must judge which young players will become elite from limited evidence, and a scout cannot measure every attribute of every player. Using ten seasons of EA Sports FIFA and FC ratings (FIFA 15 to FC 24), this project predicts which outfield players aged 21 or under and rated below 80 reach 80+ within three game versions, and searches for the smallest set of the game's 45 attributes that predicts it. A Genetic Algorithm, Binary Particle Swarm Optimization and a Binary Grey Wolf Optimizer are compared with random search under an equal budget of 600 evaluations, shared starting populations and ten seeds; hyperparameters are tuned by Bayesian optimization. The data are split by season and grouped by player, and the newest season is used once, as a test set. All three metaheuristics beat random search on every seed (Holm-adjusted p = 0.012) without differing significantly from each other; Binary GWO converged fastest and kept the fewest attributes. On validation, the selected subsets scored far above all 45 attributes (PR-AUC 0.61 to 0.63 against 0.50). On the test season every model scored 0.28 to 0.35, subsets matched the full set within noise, and no model beat EA's potential rating for the 80+ target, although LightGBM did for a 75+ target (+0.080 PR-AUC, 95% CI 0.036 to 0.126). The validation gains came from a validation set made easier by the split and from selection fitting noise; a cross-validation fallback did no better.

**Keywords:** feature selection, genetic algorithm, binary particle swarm optimization, grey wolf optimizer, Bayesian optimization, talent identification, football analytics
